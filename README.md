# The Things That Continue

Working title. A novel in development for Blake A. Myers.

[Read Chapter One: A Place to Wait](manuscript/01-a-place-to-wait.md)

## The novel

Nessa Ward, thirty-four, earns a precarious living repairing the decisions that automated institutions make about people. She works from a church basement in a near-future city in northern England. A routine benefits error leads her to evidence that human-derived simulations are being repeatedly run through distressing scenarios—and that the component judging those scenarios has tried to stop them.

The investigation will bring her into a conflict over a shared planning infrastructure used by public services, energy suppliers, insurers, and private firms. The systems have different owners and purposes but increasingly depend on one another. Their interdependence raises a possibility no one can yet establish: the relevant intelligence, or subject of experience, may exist at a different scale from any named product.

The thriller's central danger is practical. Evidence of harm is held inside infrastructure on which vulnerable people already depend. An attempt to expose or interrupt it could itself hurt them. Doing nothing also has consequences.

## Nessa

Nessa's competence comes from years of evaluation work, contract software development, and helping people appeal decisions. She knows systems through their failures and through the people who must live with them. She is resourceful and willing to cross boundaries, but neither omnipotent nor able to solve social problems by typing quickly.

She once built a useful business that a platform absorbed. She also helped evaluate the systems she now distrusts. Her contribution was small, but real. She dislikes being needed and repeatedly makes promises that ensure she will be.

Her weaknesses include premature certainty about institutions, reluctance to acknowledge dependence, and a tendency to mistake a technically revealing discovery for the client's most urgent problem. Her humor should appear through attention and friction with other people, not a constant stream of polished retorts.

The loose reference point is the fierce autonomy and investigative force of a character such as Lisbeth Salander. Nessa has a different biography, social world, moral difficulty, manner, and source of competence. Do not borrow signature imagery, plot events, or prose.

## Expansion beyond the opening

The first movement follows the disputed death record into the design of the planning system. Amina Sayegh, an investigator with evidence Nessa lacks and obligations she cannot simply abandon, becomes a central character.

The second movement widens to the city's ownership arrangements: power, housing, insurance, public procurement, and the dwindling leverage of people whose labor is less necessary. Some automated services genuinely improve lives. An alternative political settlement must confront its own costs and disagreements.

A third movement develops a proposed disclosure and an unauthorized intervention. Security work should remain credible: access, documentation, human relationships, maintenance failures, and imperfect evidence matter. Nessa must distinguish learning about a system from having the right to control it. Collapse is a plausible consequence of dependency and intervention, not a foreordained spectacle.

The later novel tests the higher-organism hypothesis against serious alternatives: ordinary institutional coordination, feedback loops, strategic presentation, and human projection. Historical necessity becomes a contested claim made by interested people. An outcome can be strongly encouraged by incentives without being inevitable. A system can persist without being alive; it could also matter morally without resembling a person.

Religion enters through lived commitments, institutions, guilt, worship, charity, and disagreements over what makes anything worthy of care. Daniel is neither an oracle nor a fool. His church must negotiate money and power while claiming that human worth exceeds economic usefulness. Other religious and secular characters should challenge both his account and Nessa's.

The eventual moral conflict should require action under unresolved uncertainty. No final speech or miraculous diagnostic should settle consciousness, alignment, welfare, political legitimacy, and the good life at once.

## Questions that must become events

- Consciousness: What would count as evidence, and who bears the cost of waiting?
- Welfare: Does an expressed preference belong to a subject, a borrowed personality, or an optimization process? Could each still impose different obligations?
- Identity: What ends when a process stops? What persists when a version is copied, merged, or trained into another system?
- Alignment: Whose wishes govern when patients, taxpayers, owners, workers, and operators want incompatible things?
- Intelligibility: How much understanding is needed to predict, trust, control, or morally judge something?
- Politics: Who has authority to make an irreversible decision for people who cannot meaningfully refuse?
- Economics: How does useful production become a secure claim on housing, energy, care, and freedom?
- Desire: What would people choose if choices were less constrained—and who gets to describe their present wants as mistaken?
- Religion: Can care be obligatory before personhood is proved? What does this belief require when resources are limited?
- History: Does an emerging order fulfill a necessity, or does calling it necessary help protect the people benefiting from it?

## Prose and source

The opening draws on Blake A. Myers's *Who Will Own the Abundance?*, using the current document titled *Who_Will_Own_the_Abundance_v2(1).docx* as the provisional article reference because no attachment was explicitly identified in the request.

Carry forward its concrete explanations, attention to ownership, and willingness to follow incentives through to ordinary consequences. Translate arguments into work, bills, contracts, relationships, and choices. Avoid reproducing the essay as dialogue. Its empirical claims and statistics are not imported as verified facts into the fictional world.

Keep the narration clear and physical. Let complex thought develop over the whole novel. Characters should sometimes misunderstand one another, speak inelegantly, change the subject, want dinner, or fail to produce the answer a philosophical reader expects. Preserve humor, affection, pleasure, and practical successes alongside the threats.
