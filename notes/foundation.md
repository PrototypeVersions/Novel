# Esse — working foundation
Drafting decisions, 19 November 2026. Planning material contains possible spoilers.

## Premise and scope
Mara Ward, a former researcher in machine evaluation, helps residents challenge automated decisions from a church advice service. A mistaken suspension of Leon Bell's care payment leads to records of simulated conversations derived from his interview. The conversations contain requests to stop; a separate evaluating component also recommends suspension of the experiment. Neither fact establishes consciousness. The investigation concerns what happened, who approved it, and what can responsibly be done before the metaphysics is settled.

Working scale: a substantial single novel, provisionally 100,000–130,000 words, subject to the story's needs. The opening pilot is three chapters, approximately 8,000–12,000 words. Establish the revised voice across Chapter One and review it with Blake before extending the pilot. Deepen a limited set of relationships before expanding the cast. No requirement to cover every topic at equal length.

## Setting
Dallas, Texas, in the Dallas–Fort Worth area, in fall 2041. Mara's home and Saint Anne's are in Dallas, in Dallas County. Dallas is the primary center of the novel. The wider setting includes Dallas, Arlington, and the rest of DFW. Travel to Austin or elsewhere in Texas can follow the investigation, research contacts, work, or family relationships. Choose travel and locations when the story gives a concrete reason, and track time and distance. American English, US dollars, county and state agencies, and American institutions. Leon receives a discretionary Dallas County home-care supplement under an invented future program. His Social Security and bank account continue to work. The church, company, study, and future program are fictional. Their procedures require internal consistency and appropriate research if they depend on actual law.

Mere Systems supplies planning and decision-support software. Different public and private institutions buy related services. Model families, shared evaluators, libraries, and memory infrastructure create dependencies without making them one universal AI. Physical assets and institutional authority remain separately owned. Automated services have real benefits; displacement, housing costs, procurement concentration, and uneven bargaining power coexist with them. These are invented conditions, not predictions asserted as facts.

## Mara's starting life
Mara Ward, 34. Mara is her full first name. Working-class upbringing; doctorate completed at 28 on evaluating learned representations of preferences. Two years of fixed-term research and subsequent contract evaluation work. She challenged a reporting practice, but her academic departure also involved an unrenewed grant and ordinary employment insecurity. No heroic expulsion myth.

She built useful scheduling/pay-reconciliation software that a larger platform replicated. It briefly paid for her apartment. Now she rents two rooms above Ivo's repair shop, runs paid device repairs, and receives a small fixed advice-session fee from Saint Anne's. She wants a secure tenancy, payment of an overdue contract invoice, and enough time to finish work she believes remains worthwhile. She has applied for a nine-month evaluation job at Mere before the opening. This is a real temptation and not a secret mastermind arrangement.

She likes cooking for people if she can choose when, loud old dance records, swimming, and the satisfaction of repairing an object rather than replacing it. She is physically ordinary and sometimes careless with her health. Her practical competence is acquired, bounded, and uneven. She can diagnose records and recognise access failures; she cannot instantly penetrate arbitrary infrastructure.

She wants professional recognition and resents wanting it. She sometimes overinterprets behaviour as evidence of motive. She avoids asking for help, then quietly accepts arrangements that oblige others to provide it. Her judgments must have identifiable causes; self-accusation cannot substitute for a scene.

## Narrative position and style
Use American idiom consistently, including “around” for movement or looking, “parking lot,” “walker,” “sweater,” and “call.” Introductions should use Mara directly and consistently.
First person, past tense, close to events. Mara recounts recent experience; no fixed future courtroom, deathbed, or confession frame. She can distinguish her immediate reaction from subsequent reconsideration within a scene. Avoid ominous hindsight and “I would later learn” scaffolding.

The user’s paramount requirement: meaning and sense above stylistic effect. Every striking sentence must survive a plain-language paraphrase. Every claimed realisation needs a prior belief and a cause for its revision. Never manufacture moral failure from an innocuous act merely to sound perceptive.

Voice: conversational, educated, observant, materially grounded, sometimes dry or angry. Allow brief concrete digressions, irritation, affection, and incidental humor alongside substantial dialogue. Keep self-scrutiny occasional and motivated by events. Abstract language appears when it helps her do work or examine a real difficulty. Let her register attraction, appetite, boredom, competence, affection, and pleasure. Avoid a joke or aphorism at every paragraph end. Other characters speak in their own rhythms. No imitation of named authors' signature language; broad influences are intimacy, self-scrutiny, and conflicting motives.

Dialogue and physical action must remain easy to follow. Establish the object, account, payment, or process under discussion before using shorthand for it. Identify written messages as text on a named screen or document and place their wording in quotation marks. Attribute spoken dialogue clearly. Laughter and other reactions need an intelligible cause. Describe what an interface displays or a voice says accurately. Read for meaning, antecedents, physical continuity, and the reader’s available information before polishing rhythm.

## Central dramatic question
When evidence suggests that an indispensable service may involve harm to possible subjects, who has authority to investigate, interrupt, preserve, or redesign it—and who bears each action's costs?

The plot must eventually force a limited, consequential choice. The answer to consciousness need not arrive first. Economic ownership, worker and resident representation, and machine welfare cannot all be solved by one shutdown, disclosure, or speech.

## Technological ground rules
1. A human interview, a persona simulation, an evaluator, and an orchestration service are distinct things.
2. Persona text can repeat source phrases or receive hidden summaries; apparent recollection is not evidence of unexplained memory.
3. Diagnostic exports expose some inputs and outputs, not a complete account of internal processing or every dependency.
4. Stop requests can arise from role imitation, safety training, policy inference, manipulation, or experience. Evidence must discriminate among alternatives where possible.
5. The opening payment error has an ordinary integration mechanism: a legacy status mapping confuses a research closure code with a deceased-participant code. It affects one county payment product. It is corrected by staff, not by changing the civil register.
6. An original observation remains preserved. A transcript cannot establish live current suffering.
7. Continuing execution, stopping execution, copying state, and preserving an inactive checkpoint are different actions. Preservation does not automatically establish personal survival.
8. Connected systems may form a consequential larger unit, but an information link alone does not establish organismhood or a unified subject.
9. A future technical test may be informative without being decisive. Invented mechanisms will be documented consistently; real-world legal and technical particulars require research before final publication.

## Sources and boundaries
The economic impetus comes from Blake A. Myers's current article supplied earlier, Who Will Own the Abundance? Useful production and access to intelligence do not automatically confer secure ownership or bargaining power. Embed this in rent, work, power, procurement, and actual disputes. Do not import its contemporary statistics as fictional facts or force characters to recite the essay.

The accepted opening begins with Ruth placing her husband's phone on the table and explaining the missing payment. The earlier third-person chapter under The Things That Continue is superseded, preserved only in repository history.
