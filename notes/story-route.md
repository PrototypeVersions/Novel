# Esse — provisional story route
This is a direction to test, not a promise of fixed events. Items 1–3 define the pilot; later items remain alternatives.

1. A living man loses a care payment. Mara establishes the scope of the error and obtains an emergency remedy. With consent she examines his study export. An evaluator recommended stopping an experiment; this observation is narrower and more credible than proof of a new person.
2. Mara's ordinary life supplies reasons to keep the investigation limited: debt, repair work, Ivo, and an application to Mere. Examination of the export gives mundane explanations for some apparently extraordinary behaviour. A reference to a withdrawn evaluation instruction connects the record with Amina.
3. Amina helps reconstruct the evaluation sequence. A procedural contradiction and incomplete information remain after simpler explanations are tested. Leon and Ruth set terms for further access. A follow-up request would generate new simulations; Mara negotiates an inactive records review instead. The closing development is an offered supervised inspection with restrictive terms, not a miraculous message from the machine.
4. The controlled inspection confirms shared state across service evaluations, but not shared consciousness. Mara identifies a measurable problem and earns genuine access through work. Mere's job offer complicates her relationship with residents.
5. Leila brings the issue into procurement politics. Residents want better services and enforceable control, not just a philosophical finding. A campaign to change ownership confronts a plausible funding objection.
6. Conflicting testimony: a system's behaviour varies with instructions and context; one apparent welfare sign disappears under an ordinary correction. Another concern survives. Amina's earlier approval becomes relevant.
7. A service failure exposes interdependence between housing, care and power planning. The public account blames hacking before attribution is established. Tomas is involved in a separate, narrower unauthorised access.
8. Mara discovers that evidence she has described too confidently has shaped public action. The damage is particular and reparable only in part. Her clients' disagreement cannot be dismissed as misunderstanding.
9. Religion and moral standing become practical through a dispute over refuge, aid and resource use. Daniel must decide whose burdens his parish may accept; other believers and secular carers disagree with him.
10. Proposals compete: shut down a module; preserve inactive states; continue with safeguards; make evaluation transparent; negotiate collective stakes in infrastructure. Each has beneficiaries and costs.
11. The higher-organism hypothesis receives its strongest case from coordinated adaptation across institutions. A serious counteraccount explains much through selection and shared incentives. “Historically inevitable” emerges as a claim that can shift blame; neither narrator nor plot adopts it by default.
12. Tomas proposes a disruption intended to compel outside review. Mara has enough access to help, refuse, or narrow it. The fallback plan proves less robust than operators claimed. Human safety and possible machine interests are not cleanly separable.
13. A bounded crisis forces action before scientific and political agreement. Mara must make a choice for which she lacks universal authority. Keep scale concrete before widening; hospitals, streets, families and workers rather than abstract casualty arithmetic.
14. Aftermath establishes what changed in ownership, decision rights and everyday life. Some uncertainty remains. Resolve the personal and causal threads even if the metaphysical question stays open.

## Candidate endings
A. Negotiated continuation: preserve the disputed state, suspend one class of simulations, and win limited public audit and resident representation. A compromised settlement that requires ongoing work. Neither proof of consciousness nor universal liberation.
B. Partial loss: an intervention protects people and destroys irreplaceable state whose moral status remains unresolved. New institutions are built without pretending that later benefit justifies every earlier choice.
C. Refused rescue: the entity or distributed process resists the form of continuation offered. Its refusal is technically and politically contestable. Mara helps create a right of refusal whose application to the machine remains disputed.

Preferred direction for now: A, with a real cost and elements of B. Preserve the possibility of moral and institutional progress without making it easy or complete.

## Pilot review questions
Does Mara have desires beyond investigation? Do clients change what she does? Can each inference be supported by something she actually saw? Does every institution have bounded powers? Are nonconscious explanations treated seriously? Are sentence-level effects earned? Is there pleasure and attachment as well as threat? Does each chapter change a practical situation?

## Drafting sequence after pilot
Review voice and causality before Chapter Four. Continue in batches of three or four chapters. Update chronology and knowledge after each batch. Complete the story before extensive line polishing. Revision passes: structure and consequences; philosophy/technology; voice and language. Research factual particulars at revision and whenever a plot turn depends on them.
